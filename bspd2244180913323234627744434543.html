Background

A $7.5B telecom with a borrowed identity.

Brightspeed is the nation's fifth-largest ILEC, valued at over $7.5B, expanding its fiber network to reach 1 million new connections by 2023. As a spin-off of Lumen and CenturyLink, it serves 6.5 million locations across the southeast, southwest, and great lakes regions.

After Apollo Global's acquisition in November 2021, the website was a patchwork of Lumen and CenturyLink pages. Enterprise clients searching for fiber solutions were bouncing immediately, not because the product was wrong, but because the experience signalled the wrong company entirely.

The problem

Three brands on one site, and customers were leaving.

  • Enterprise buyers met Lumen navigation, CenturyLink terminology, and Brightspeed branding on the same page
  • IA built for residential buyers, completely wrong for enterprise and wholesale segments
  • Support pages were dead ends, no routing by segment, no clear next step
  • Sales avoided sending prospects to specific pages, they couldn't predict the brand they'd see
  • Existing users dropping off, adding to client attrition
Brand fragmentationHigh bounceLost pipeline
The solution

Migrate first, then redesign.

  • CGI's HCx team brought in to migrate cloned Lumen pages to Brightspeed.com and build the 2.0 experience
  • Phase 1: migrate and launch by October 2022, get Brightspeed branding live
  • Phase 2: redesign the B2B experience end to end for enterprise conversion
  • AEM-based CMS, all components built to work within platform constraints
  • Focus: increase acquisition, reduce attrition through a better experience

Competitive analysis

How Spectrum, Verizon, AT&T, and Google Fiber do it.

The finding that changed everything: Spectrum, AT&T, and Verizon Business all led with outcomes, "keep your team connected," "solutions built for your business." Lumen led with bandwidth numbers. Enterprise buyers don't evaluate by spec first, so we restructured Brightspeed's whole messaging hierarchy around this.

The full competitive teardown across four providers.

Key insights

Three findings that changed the direction.

The workshops and competitive analysis weren't process theatre. Each finding below led directly to a design decision.

01

Enterprise and SMB shared one landing page

Analytics showed completely different scroll patterns and exit points. Enterprise buyers dropped at product pages; SMB dropped at pricing. Same page, very different intents.

02

Competitors lead with outcomes, not specs

Spectrum and AT&T both led with "keep your team connected." Lumen led with bandwidth numbers. We shifted Brightspeed's messaging to match how enterprise buyers actually evaluate.

03

Sales couldn't hand off URLs with confidence

The sales team avoided sending prospects to specific pages because they couldn't predict the brand they'd see. A consistent experience was a sales-enablement problem, not just a UX one.

Mapping what was working, what wasn't, and what needed to change.

2.0 design system

15+ components, built to last.

I owned the brand guidelines and the new Figma design system, migrated from Sketch. More than 15 components were built across the engagement. The hardest was the contact bar, surfacing Sales, Support, Enterprise, and SMB paths in a 64px strip that worked across every AEM breakpoint. Eight iterations across three sprints before sign-off.

Guidelines, typography, color, spacing, and components.

15 Scrum sprints

Iterative delivery across 15 two-week sprints.

In Sprint 6, the client pushed back on the segment dropdown for the support page, they felt it added a step. We came back the next session with Day 1 analytics: 85% of users abandoned the support page without taking any action. The dropdown shipped. That's how every sprint worked, decisions backed by data, not opinion.

All tasks were broken into Jira stories across designers, writers, and developers, with a clear priority order. Four sessions per sprint: research, concepts, iteration, sign-off.

Key decisions

Three decisions that mattered most.

90 days, 150 pages, a distributed team. These three kept the experience coherent when everything was pushing toward shortcuts.

01

Segment-first IA, not product-first

  • Enterprise buyers identify as Enterprise, SMB, or Wholesale before they think about products
  • Analytics confirmed enterprise and SMB had completely different drop-off on the same page
  • So we restructured the entire IA around buyer segment
47% more page views
02

A segment dropdown over wall-of-text support

  • Inherited support pages listed every contact option for every segment in 400 words
  • I pushed for a dropdown as the first interaction, controversial with the client
  • One dropdown, three paths: you tell us who you are, we show what you need
Support drop-off eliminated
03

Design system before touching any pages

  • Sprints 1 and 2 went to Figma tokens, components, and naming, before any migration
  • Components built ad-hoc across 150 pages would have fragmented the brand again
  • Two sprints on architecture saved ten sprints of rework
Zero rework loops in Phase 2

Outcomes

Two numbers that mattered.

Day 2 analytics confirmed the segment-first approach worked. These aren't site-wide rising-tide effects, they track directly to specific decisions made in specific sprints.

50%
Bounce drop on Business Solutions, the page that mattered most
Why this number
  • Business Solutions was the first page enterprise buyers hit from paid search, the highest-stakes redesign
  • Day 1: Lumen branding, no routing, high abandonment. Day 2: Brightspeed brand, segment paths, clear CTAs
  • The drop ties directly to one decision, routing buyers to their segment before showing products
47%
More page views site-wide, Day 1 to Day 2
The broader picture
  • 70% bounce drop across all /ew/ pages, brand coherence, not just one page fix
  • March and April 2023 saw the highest Marketo lead volume, Day 2 months consistently outperformed Day 1
  • Day 2 converted higher, meaning a business agreed to a Brightspeed contract
