Background

A $7.5B telecom with a borrowed identity.

Brightspeed is the nation's fifth-largest ILEC, valued at over $7.5B, expanding its fiber optics network to reach 1 million new connections by 2023. As a spin-off of Lumen and CenturyLink, it serves 6.5 million locations in the southeast, southwest, and great lakes regions.

After Apollo Global's acquisition in November 2021, the website was a patchwork of Lumen and CenturyLink pages. Enterprise clients searching for fiber solutions were bouncing immediately — not because the product was wrong, but because the experience signalled the wrong company entirely.

The Problem

Three brands. Zero coherence. Real churn.

  • Enterprise buyers encountered Lumen navigation, CenturyLink terminology, and Brightspeed branding on the same page
  • IA built for residential buyers — completely wrong for enterprise and wholesale segments
  • Support pages were dead ends — no routing by segment, no clear next step
  • Sales avoided sending prospects to specific pages — couldn't predict what brand they'd see
  • Existing users dropping off, contributing to client attrition
Brand fragmentationHigh bounceLost pipeline
The Solution

Migrate, then transform. Coherence first.

  • CGI's HCx team brought in to migrate cloned Lumen pages to Brightspeed.com and build the 2.0 experience
  • Phase 1: migrate and launch by October 2022 — get Brightspeed branding live
  • Phase 2: redesign the B2B experience end-to-end for enterprise conversion
  • AEM-based CMS — all components built to work within platform constraints
  • Focus: increase customer acquisition, reduce attrition through improved CX

Competitive Analysis

How Spectrum, Verizon,
AT&T, and Google Fiber do it.

The finding that changed everything: Spectrum, AT&T, and Verizon Business all led with outcomes — "keep your team connected," "solutions built for your business." Lumen led with bandwidth numbers. Enterprise buyers don't evaluate by spec first. We restructured Brightspeed's entire messaging hierarchy around this.

Key Insights & Opportunities

Three findings that changed the design direction.

Rose-Thorn-Bud workshops and competitive analysis weren't process theatre. Each finding below directly influenced a design decision.

🔍

Enterprise and SMB buyers shared one landing page

Analytics showed completely different scroll patterns and exit points — enterprise buyers dropped at product pages; SMB dropped at pricing. Same page, fundamentally different intents.

📊

Competitors lead with outcomes, not specs

Spectrum and AT&T both led with "keep your team connected." Lumen led with bandwidth numbers. We shifted Brightspeed's messaging hierarchy to match how enterprise buyers actually evaluate.

⚡

Sales couldn't hand off URLs with confidence

The sales team confirmed they avoided sending prospects to specific pages because they couldn't predict what brand they'd see. A consistent experience was a sales enablement problem, not just UX.

Working Sessions & 15 Scrum Sprints

Iterative delivery across 15 two-week sprints.

In Sprint 6, session 2, the client pushed back on the segment dropdown on the support page. They argued it added an extra step. We came back session 3 with the Day 1 analytics: 85% of users were abandoning the support page without taking any action. The dropdown shipped. That's how every sprint worked — decisions backed by data, not opinion.

All tasks broken into Jira stories across designers, writers, and developers. Priority order: Business landing page first, then segment pages, product pages, support last. 4 sessions per sprint — research, concepts, iteration, sign-off.

Critical Design Decisions

Three calls that shaped the experience.

90 days, 150 pages, a distributed team. These three kept the experience coherent when everything was pushing toward shortcuts.

01

Segment-first IA, not product-first

Enterprise buyers identify as Enterprise, SMB, or Wholesale before they think about products. Restructured the entire IA around buyer segment — analytics confirmed enterprise and SMB had completely different drop-off patterns on the same page.

↑ 47% more page views
02

Segment dropdown over wall-of-text support

The inherited support pages listed every contact option for every segment in 400 words. Pushed for a dropdown as the first interaction — controversial with the client. One dropdown, three paths. You tell us who you are, we show you what you need.

→ Support drop-off eliminated
03

Design system before touching any pages

Spent sprints 1–2 on Figma tokens, component library, and naming conventions before any page migration. Components built ad-hoc across 150 pages would have fragmented the brand just as badly as what we inherited.

→ Zero rework loops in Phase 2
