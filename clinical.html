Project Overview

Turning one-off dashboards into a scalable practice.

Over several clinical analytics initiatives at Inova, I partnered with clinical leaders, BI developers, analysts, and executives to improve the usability, consistency, and trust of dashboards used for decision-making.

Instead of treating each dashboard as a one-off design, I helped create a scalable approach — combining dashboard standardization, data storytelling, design system adoption, and governance into a repeatable process that any team could follow.

The Challenge

Dashboards built in isolation, trust eroding.

Clinical analytics teams were building dashboards independently — creating inconsistent experiences across tools, teams, and service lines.

  • Different layouts, filters, KPI styles, and terminology
  • Metric definitions not always visible or consistent
  • Users struggled to connect process → outcomes → cost
  • Developers rebuilt similar patterns repeatedly
  • Trust dropped when dashboards looked and behaved differently
The Solution

A shared design language that scales.

A design system and governance process that could scale across multiple analytics products — not just better dashboards, but a better way of building them.

  • Facilitated discovery workshops and working sessions
  • Created lo-fi concepts and hi-fi dashboard frameworks
  • Built Figma standards for Tableau dashboards
  • Designed annotation layers, KPI patterns, filters, layouts
  • Created a repeatable intake, design review, and delivery process
My Process

From discovery to repeatable dashboard standards.

A structured UX workflow moved vague dashboard requests into reusable design standards and development-ready concepts. Each step produced an artifact — nothing stayed in someone's head.

Discovery & Insights

Three themes that shaped everything.

Through workshops, dashboard audits, and stakeholder reviews, we surfaced the patterns behind the frustration. Each theme became a pillar of the design system.

📐

Standardization

Dashboards needed a consistent look, structure, and interaction model across teams — same layout logic, same KPI patterns, same filter behavior.

📖

Data Storytelling

Users needed dashboards to explain what matters, not just display more numbers. Context, annotations, and guided interpretation were missing everywhere.

🔒

Trust & Governance

Metric definitions, data ownership, refresh schedules, and source context needed to be easier to find — without them, clinical users didn't trust what they saw.

The design system gave teams a shared language — defining spacing, typography, colors, KPI components, annotation patterns, filters, and dashboard structure. Built in Figma, validated against Tableau's rendering constraints, and documented for handoff to developers who don't always sit next to a designer.

A clinical analytics dashboard suite tracking Heart Failure performance across Length of Stay, Mortality, and Readmission — designed from intake through five working sessions to a final development-ready Tableau experience.

Step 1 — Intake & Discovery
Intake · Discovery

Working Session Notes & Early Data Review

Captured goals, research questions, and success criteria — alongside the 2025 Target Modeling Tool showing LOS O/E performance across peer groups. Identified key questions around improvement targets, benchmarking logic, and filter requirements.

Step 2 — Working Session 5: Scope
Working Session 5 · Scope

Dashboard Architecture — Three Clinical Domains

Defined the full scope across LOS, Mortality, and Readmission — each requiring Hospital-level and Service Line-level views with alternate measure tables. This session locked the page structure, tab logic, and filter requirements before hi-fidelity work began.

Step 3 — User Flow & Mid-Fidelity Design
Lo-Fi · Mid-Fidelity

User Flow Wireframe & Mid-Fidelity Dashboard

3-page user flow mapping alternative measures across Hospital LOS, Hospital Mortality, and System Readmission. Mid-fidelity shows the custom measure definitions panel, peer group filters, and hospital performance tables with 12-month improvement tracking.

Step 4 — Final Hi-Fidelity Design
Final Hi-Fidelity · Dev-Ready

Complete Dashboard Suite — LOS · Mortality · Readmissions

Three-panel final design: LOS O/E & Opportunity Days, Mortality O/E & Lives Saved per 1,000 Cases, and Readmissions — each showing facility performance against AMC, community, and complex care peer groups across 12-month index, top quartile, and top decile benchmarks.

Governance Framework

Standardizing intake, design review, and delivery.

A lightweight governance process ensures future dashboards follow a consistent path from request to implementation — so the standards don't only apply to dashboards I personally designed, but to every dashboard the team builds going forward.

Step 1

Request

Clarify the business need, audience, and decision it supports.

Step 2

Discovery

Identify workflows, metrics, and decisions the dashboard needs to enable.

Step 3

Wireframes

Align on layout and structure before any development begins.

Step 4

Review

Validate with clinical and BI teams against the design system standards.

Step 5

Develop

Build using approved standards, templates, and component patterns.

Outcomes

A scalable foundation for clinical analytics.

The value here wasn't in designing individual dashboards — it was in helping teams align on how dashboards should be structured, reviewed, and delivered. The system outlasts any single engagement.

This project showed how UX can create impact beyond interface design. By combining workshops, design systems, dashboard UX, and governance, I helped clinical and BI teams create analytics experiences that were easier to understand, easier to build, and easier to trust. The most durable contribution wasn't any single screen — it was the shared process that lets teams build better dashboards without me in the room.

Reflection

UX impact beyond the interface.

The challenge in healthcare analytics isn't usually the visual design — it's building shared vocabulary across clinical, BI, and leadership teams who all define the same metric differently. The design system forced that conversation early, before anyone had written a line of Tableau code.

Key Takeaway

The system is the product.

The most durable UX contribution here wasn't any individual screen — it was the shared process, vocabulary, and design standards that allow multiple teams to build better dashboards independently, with less rework and more confidence in the result. That compounds over time in a way individual deliverables don't.
