Project Overview

Turning one-off dashboards into a scalable practice

Over several clinical analytics initiatives at Inova Health System, I partnered with clinical leaders, implementation teams, BI developers, analysts, and executives to improve the usability, consistency, and trust of dashboards used for decision-making. Instead of treating each dashboard as a one-off design, I helped create a scalable approach for dashboard standardization data storytelling design system adoption and governance.

The Challenge

Dashboards built in isolation, trust eroding

Clinical analytics teams were building dashboards independently, which created inconsistent experiences across tools, teams, and service lines.

  • Different layouts, filters, KPI styles, and terminology
  • Metric definitions were not always visible or consistent
  • Users struggled to connect process → outcomes → cost
  • Developers rebuilt similar dashboard patterns repeatedly
  • Trust dropped when dashboards looked and behaved differently
The Solution

A shared design language that scales

I worked with BI and clinical teams to establish reusable dashboard patterns and a design system that could scale across multiple analytics products.

  • Facilitated discovery workshops and working sessions
  • Created low-fidelity concepts and high-fidelity dashboard frameworks
  • Built Figma standards for Tableau dashboards
  • Designed annotation layers, KPI patterns, tables, filters, and layouts
  • Created a repeatable process for intake, design review, and governance

Example 1

Heart Failure — Target Modeling Tool

A clinical analytics dashboard suite tracking Heart Failure performance across Length of Stay, Mortality, and Readmission — designed from intake through five working sessions to a final development-ready Tableau experience.

LOS O/E LOS Opportunity Days Mortality O/E Lives Saved per 1,000 Cases Readmissions Peer Benchmarking
Step 1 — Intake & Discovery
Intake · Discovery

Working Session Notes & Early Data Review

Captured goals, research questions, and success criteria — alongside the 2025 Target Modeling Tool showing LOS O/E performance across AMC, community, and complex care peer groups. Identified key questions around improvement targets, benchmarking logic, and filter requirements.

Step 2 — Working Session 5 Scope
Working Session 5 · Scope

Dashboard Architecture — Three Clinical Domains

Defined the full scope across three domains — Length of Stay, Mortality, and Readmission — each requiring Hospital-level and Service Line-level views with alternate measure definition tables. This session locked the page structure, tab logic, and filter requirements before hi-fidelity work began.

Step 3 — User Flow & Mid-Fidelity Design
Lo-Fi · Mid-Fidelity

User Flow Wireframe & Mid-Fidelity Dashboard

Left: 3-page user flow mapping alternative measures (LOS Opportunity Days, Hospital Mortality O/E, Readmission O/E) across each page — with opportunity days and saved metrics surfaced at each level. Right: mid-fidelity design with the custom measure definitions panel open, peer group filters, and hospital performance tables showing 12-month improvement tracking against benchmarks.

Step 4 — Final Hi-Fidelity Design
Final Hi-Fidelity · Dev-Ready

Complete Dashboard Suite — LOS · Mortality · Readmissions

Three-panel final design built in Tableau: LOS O/E & Opportunity Days, Mortality O/E & Lives Saved per 1,000 Cases, and Readmissions — each showing facility-level performance against AMC, community, and complex care peer groups across 12-month index, improved index, top quartile, and top decile benchmarks. Includes dynamic peer group filters and alternate measure definition tables per CCS category.

This work helped shift dashboard design from isolated one-off builds into a more consistent, scalable, and governed analytics experience. The value was not only in designing individual dashboards, but in helping teams align on how dashboards should be structured, reviewed, and delivered.

Reflection

UX impact beyond the interface

This project showed how UX can create impact beyond interface design. By combining workshops, design systems, dashboard UX, and governance, I helped clinical and BI teams create analytics experiences that were easier to understand, easier to build, and easier to trust.

Key Takeaway

The system is the product

The most durable UX contribution wasn't any single screen - it was the shared process, vocabulary, and design standards that allowed multiple teams to build better dashboards independently, with less rework and more confidence in the result.
