The Problem

The tool worked.
The data governance didn't.

ChatGPT was genuinely useful for Inova employees. Drafting clinical summaries, responding to operational queries, processing documents. Nobody was using it carelessly. But it had no HIPAA compliance, no PHI protection, and no visibility for Inova's compliance teams. So it got blocked. Blocking it didn't remove the need. The approved alternative, SHARP, couldn't handle a clinical note. The gap between approved AI and actually useful AI was the problem I was asked to close.

Why ChatGPT was blocked
🚫

Inova legally cannot use it for patient-adjacent work without a signed Business Associate Agreement.
👁️

Discovery

What the workshops revealed.

I ran discovery workshops with clinical staff, IT, compliance, and operations leads. Three findings shaped every design decision that followed.

📋

SHARP's limitations were specific and fixable

SHARP had one real problem: context window. Clinical notes, policy documents, and operational summaries are long. SHARP couldn't handle them. That wasn't a model problem -it was a configuration one. Inova Assist would use a higher-capacity model and expose it properly.

🔒

Compliance needed to be felt, not just stated

A footer disclaimer wasn't enough. Users needed to actively acknowledge what the tool was and wasn't for before they started typing. The compliance gate had to be a UX decision, not a legal one -clear, specific, and impossible to accidentally skip.

⚡

Speed determined whether people would use it

If the governed tool felt slower than what employees remembered from ChatGPT, adoption would fail immediately. The LLM switcher -giving users a Fast model and a Thinking model -was the answer to "why use this instead?"

Feature Scoping

What we built first -and why the order mattered.

ServiceNow ticket creation was the most requested workflow from discovery. It was also the most complex API dependency. I pushed to keep it as Phase 2 -not because it wasn't important, but because shipping a broken workflow automation would have destroyed trust in the rest of the product at launch. The chat experience had to be flawless first. That's the only way you earn the right to add complexity.

Critical Design Decisions

Three decisions that made this
more than a chatbot wrapper.

Wrapping a ChatGPT API in a text box takes an afternoon. Designing a tool that healthcare employees trust enough to use instead of the one they were already using that's the actual problem. These three decisions are what separated Inova Assist from SHARP.

01

Examples / Capabilities / Limitations on every empty state

The first thing a user sees on a new chat isn't a blank input -it's a 3-column grid showing what the AI can do, what it remembers, and where it gets things wrong. Healthcare-specific examples: wound care protocols, managing complex patients. This replaced a training session. Users knew what to ask from the first session without ever reading a manual.

→ Users calibrated expectations immediately
02

Terms of Use as a blocking modal -four specific bullets, not a wall of text

Compliance initially wanted a footer link. I pushed for a blocking modal on first use with four specific bullets: no PHI, no sensitive data, AI may be wrong, HIPAA applies. Accept or Cancel -no skipping. The argument: a footer link gets ignored. A blocking modal with specific bullets creates genuine acknowledgement. Legal signed off after one conversation once they saw the design.

→ Zero ambiguity about what's permitted
03

LLM switcher always visible -not buried in settings

Fast (4.1) for quick queries. Thinking (5.0) for complex analysis. I put the switcher at the top of every screen rather than in a settings panel. If users don't know they can switch models, they'll assume the AI is always slow or always fast and calibrate their expectations wrong. Transparency about the model you're using is a trust feature, not a power-user feature.

→ Users matched model to task deliberately

Usability Testing Results

Two numbers.
Both from testing.

We ran two rounds of moderated usability testing with 8 participants each -a mix of clinical staff, operations, and IT. These are the two numbers worth talking about.

91%
Task completion rate -up from 68% in round one
What changed between round 1 and round 2
  • →Round 1 drop-off was at the Terms of Use modal -users weren't sure if "Cancel" meant end the session or decline the terms. Relabelled to Accept / Decline. Drop-off at that step went to zero.
  • →LLM switcher confusion: users were ignoring version numbers (4.1, 5.0) entirely. Adding "Fast" and "Thinking" as labels fixed comprehension immediately.
  • →Document upload was hard to find. Moving the paperclip icon to a more prominent position in the input bar resolved it in one iteration.
8/8
Participants said they would use Inova Assist instead of workarounds
How we asked it
  • →End-of-session question: "Would you use Inova Assist for this task going forward, or would you find another way?" All 8 said yes in round 2 -compared to 5 out of 8 in round 1.
  • →The 3 who said no in round 1 all cited the same issue: the Terms of Use friction and not understanding the LLM switcher. Both fixed before round 2.
  • →This is the metric that matters -the product only works if it replaces the workarounds, not just if users can complete tasks in a lab setting.

Designing AI for healthcare isn't about making the most powerful tool. It's about making the most trustworthy one. Every decision in Inova Assist was a trust decision first -the compliance gate, the limitations disclosure, the concern reporting, the model transparency. None of these were legal requirements. They were the reason employees would use a governed tool instead of an ungoverned one.

What I'd do differently

Start the ServiceNow integration earlier.

Deferring it to Phase 2 was technically right but strategically costly. ServiceNow ticket creation was the single workflow that would have driven the most immediate, visible value for clinical staff -the thing they missed most when ChatGPT was blocked. If I could redo the timeline, I'd have started the API discovery in parallel with design rather than sequentially. We lost two sprints we didn't need to lose.

The bigger lesson

In regulated environments, compliance is the product.

The Terms of Use modal, the non-medical use reminder, the concern reporting -these aren't overhead. They're the features that make the tool usable at work. Without them, you don't have a healthcare AI product. You have a chatbot that happens to run inside a hospital network. The difference matters -to legal, to compliance, and to the employees who need to trust what they're typing into it.
