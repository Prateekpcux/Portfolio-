BACKGROUND
Brightspeed is the nation's fifth largest ILEC valued at over 7.5 billion USD. It is expanding its fiber optics network to reach 1 million new connections by 2023. As a spin-off of Lumen and CenturyLink, it serves 6.5 million locations in rural and suburban areas. Brightspeed focuses on fiber-based telecom solutions for B2C and B2B markets in the southeast, southwest, and great lakes regions.
THE PROBLEM
In November 2021, Apollo Global Management (A hedgefund) struck a deal with Lumen Technologies to acquire Brightspeed. After the acquisition, the website experience felt disjointed due to branding associated with Lumen and CenturyLink webpages, as well as subpar navigation and support pages. As a result, a lot of existing users dropped off, leading to client attrition. Since then the organiztion has been affected with existing users dropping of the service and client attrition.
THE SOLUTION
As of March 2022, CGI's HCx team was introduced to oversee the relationship with Brightspeed clients and assist in migrating "cloned pages" from Lumen pages. Additionally, to enhance the Brightspeed Business experience after migration, we were involved in redesigning their existing and new landing pages and product pages which was built using Adobe Experience Manager (AEM).
The org's focus was on increasing customer acquisition while reducing customer attrition through improved CX with the launch of new products.
MY ROLE & ROADMAP
I joined the team as senior product designer and worked alongside a UX writer, UX strategist and supervised two UX designers for the whole migration effort. My team was brought on to help the new transition from Lumen (formally CenturyLink) to Brightspeed. We immediately had two main goals that would span close to a years worth of work.
  • Help with the migration of "cloned pages" from Lumen.com and Centurylink.com to the new Brightspeed.com Business experience and launch by October 2022.
  • Build a new 2.0 website experience for Brightspeed and launch mid January 2023.

BACKGROUND
Periscope is an enterprise analytics marketplace designed for Inova Health System to help users discover trusted dashboards, reports, AI models, extracts, and data products across the organization.

Inova’s analytics ecosystem included assets spread across platforms such as Power BI, Tableau, Epic, SharePoint, and internal AI tools. Users needed a centralized experience to improve discoverability, transparency, ownership, and governance.
THE PROBLEM
Analytics assets were fragmented across multiple systems, making it difficult for users to find the right dashboards, understand ownership, identify certified assets, and avoid duplicate work.

Users often relied on tribal knowledge instead of a centralized, trusted discovery experience.
THE SOLUTION
We designed a centralized marketplace experience where users could search, browse, filter, favorite, and request access to trusted analytics and AI assets.

The platform surfaced metadata, ownership, certification status, related assets, and governance information to improve trust and usability.
MY ROLE & ROADMAP
I worked as the Senior UX/UI Designer for Periscope, partnering with Data Governance, Analytics, AI, and Engineering teams.

My responsibilities included:
  • Facilitating stakeholder workshops and discovery sessions
  • Defining information architecture and taxonomy
  • Designing homepage, browse, details, favorites, campaigns, and AI inventory experiences
  • Creating a scalable metadata and filter framework
  • Supporting developer handoff and design reviews
OUR APPROACH & DIRECTION
DEFINING THE CHALLENGE
BUILDING AN ENTERPRISE ANALYTICS MARKETPLACE
Before designing the experience, we needed to understand how users searched for analytics assets, what metadata mattered, and how governance information should appear in the user journey.
  • Stakeholder workshops
  • Analytics asset inventory review
  • Taxonomy and metadata discussions
  • Search and filter strategy
  • AI inventory discovery
  • Prototype reviews and iterations
DISCOVERY & KEY INSIGHTS
Through stakeholder interviews and working sessions, we identified that users needed a simpler way to find trusted assets without knowing which platform or team owned them.

Key opportunities included improving search, surfacing certification status, grouping assets by meaningful categories, and making ownership visible.
INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE
One of the largest design challenges was organizing dashboards, reports, AI models, extracts, and documentation into a scalable taxonomy.

We structured the experience around service lines, data domains, sites of care, professional services, tool type, asset status, and certification.
RESULTS
FINAL DESIGNS
The final experience included a search-first homepage, browse all assets page, detailed asset views, favorites, campaigns, and a dedicated AI inventory.
OUTCOMES
Periscope created a foundation for enterprise analytics discovery and governance at Inova.

  • Centralized analytics discovery across multiple platforms
  • Improved visibility into ownership and certification
  • Standardized metadata and filtering framework
  • Created a dedicated inventory for AI solutions
  • Reduced reliance on tribal knowledge for finding trusted assets

This project demonstrated how UX can transform data governance into a more accessible, transparent, and user-centered experience.
