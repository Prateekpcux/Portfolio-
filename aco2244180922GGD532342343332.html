Background

A voluntary federal healthcare coordination program

The Shared Savings Program encourages groups of doctors, hospitals, and other healthcare providers to come together as ACOs to give coordinated, high-quality care to their Medicare beneficiaries. It's administered by CMS, a federal agency within the US Department of Health and Human Services (HHS).

Background

A voluntary federal program with growing scale

The Shared Savings Program had grown dramatically, demanding a system that could support annual program requirement changes for nearly 513 ACO organizations. The existing workflows were outdated and creating friction at every compliance and reporting touchpoint.

Program Growth
The Problem

A growing program outpacing its system

The scope of the Shared Savings Program grew significantly. The existing system needed to support annual changes to program requirements for nearly 513 ACO organizations. The Registration and NOIA application processes were tedious, time-consuming, and complex -users struggled to get verified and access the system.

The Solution

A redesigned, seamless user flow

A full experience redesign to create an intuitive and improved flow meeting the growing needs of program administrators, ACO leaders, and healthcare providers -with a specific focus on the Landing Page, NOIA Application, and Registration redesign.

My Role

Primary UX designer -leading the team for 3+ years

I served as the primary UX designer for the project, leading the team for a duration exceeding 3 years. I worked with a UX designer and researcher to gain insights into the challenges users face when searching, browsing, and managing visual complexity across the platform.

Contributions

End-to-end UX ownership: stakeholder research, user interviews, personas, user flows, wireframes, branding, UI style guide, hi-fidelity prototypes, usability testing, 508 compliance, and developer handoff across multiple product iterations.

Defining the Challenge

Landing Page, NOIA Application & Registration Redesign

The Registration and NOIA application are two different processes for users to get verified and access the system. Users found the process very tedious, time-consuming, and complex. The goal was to make the process simple to navigate and get access to the system without waiting.

Key Insights

What we learned by asking why

We interviewed end users to understand their frustration from the existing flow. With their feedback, we developed a new user flow to reduce complexity. A few of the core insights:

Process was too long

Registration and NOIA verification required users to navigate too many disconnected steps with unclear progress signals.

Waiting without feedback

Users had no visibility into where their application stood -no status indicators, no estimated timelines.

Visual complexity overload

The existing system lacked padding, spacing, and hierarchy -making it hard to focus or scan key information.

Branding

Visual design aligned with CMS culture

Once wireframes were validated, I focused on branding with two goals:

1. Match CMS's culture. I wanted ACO-MS to feel approachable and associated with CMS. I incorporated colors matching the CMS color palette while giving it a new look and feel -created using Adobe Color.

2. Fix visual clarity. The system lacked proper padding and text spacing, making it difficult for users to focus on content. A total visual overhaul was needed to make the experience useful, meaningful, and enjoyable.

Program Performance Results

Measurable impact on Medicare beneficiaries

The platform redesign contributed to significant program growth. According to CMS 2022 performance results, there were 66 new enrollments, 140 renewals, and over 11 million beneficiaries receiving care -a 3% growth of 324K compared to the prior year. In 2019, ACO-MS users saved $1.2 billion in Medicare, up from $739M the prior year.
