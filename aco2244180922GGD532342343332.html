Background

A voluntary federal program that coordinates care for millions.

The Medicare Shared Savings Program encourages groups of doctors, hospitals, and other healthcare providers to come together as ACOs — Accountable Care Organizations — to give coordinated, high-quality care to their Medicare beneficiaries. It's administered by CMS, a federal agency within HHS.

The program had grown dramatically, demanding a platform that could support annual requirement changes for 500+ ACO organizations. The existing workflows were outdated, creating friction at every compliance and reporting touchpoint.

The Problem

A growing program outpacing its own system.

The Registration and NOIA application processes were tedious, time-consuming, and complex — users struggled to get verified and access the system. No status visibility. No clear progress. Disconnected steps at every touchpoint.

High abandonmentNo status visibilityComplex flows
The Solution

A redesigned, seamless user flow for every touchpoint.

A full experience redesign — intuitive flows meeting the needs of program administrators, ACO leaders, and healthcare providers. Specific focus on the Landing Page, NOIA Application, and Registration redesign.

Defining the Challenge

Landing Page, NOIA Application
& Registration Redesign.

The Registration and NOIA application are two different processes for users to get verified and access the system. Users found the process very tedious, time-consuming, and complex. The goal was to make it simple to navigate — getting access to the system without unnecessary waiting.

Key Insights

Three findings that changed the design direction.

We interviewed end users to understand their frustration with the existing flow. Each insight below directly influenced a design decision.

⏱️

Process was too long

Registration and NOIA verification required users to navigate too many disconnected steps with unclear progress signals — users didn't know how far they were from done.

🔍

Waiting without feedback

Users had no visibility into where their application stood — no status indicators, no estimated timelines. The silence after submission created anxiety and repeat contacts to support.

📊

Visual complexity overload

The existing system lacked padding, spacing, and hierarchy — making it hard to focus or scan key information on dense federal forms.

Branding

Visual design aligned
with CMS culture.

Two goals: match CMS's existing brand culture so ACO-MS felt like a trusted government product, and fix the visual clarity problems that made the existing system exhausting to use. I used Adobe Color to derive a palette from CMS's official color standards, then systematically applied spacing, type hierarchy, and component rules the existing system had never had.

Program Performance Results

Measurable impact on Medicare beneficiaries.

According to CMS 2022 performance results, the platform supported 66 new enrollments, 140 renewals, and over 11 million beneficiaries receiving care — a 3% growth of 324K vs the prior year. The redesign contributed directly to this growth by removing the registration barriers that were preventing new ACOs from onboarding.
