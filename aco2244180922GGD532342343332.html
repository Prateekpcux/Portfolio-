Background

A voluntary federal program that coordinates care for millions.

The Medicare Shared Savings Program encourages groups of doctors, hospitals, and other providers to come together as ACOs, Accountable Care Organizations, to give coordinated, high-quality care to their Medicare beneficiaries. It's administered by CMS, a federal agency within HHS.

The program had grown dramatically, demanding a platform that could absorb annual requirement changes for 500+ ACO organizations. The existing workflows were outdated, creating friction at every compliance and reporting touchpoint.

The problem

A growing program outpacing its own system.

The Registration and NOIA application processes were tedious, time-consuming, and complex. Users struggled to get verified and into the system. No status visibility, no clear progress, disconnected steps at every touchpoint.

High abandonmentNo status visibilityComplex flows
The solution

A redesigned, seamless flow for every touchpoint.

A full experience redesign with intuitive flows that meet the needs of program administrators, ACO directors, and healthcare providers. Focused on the Landing Page, NOIA Application, and Registration redesign.

Picturing the target audience

Two user types. One platform. Different mental models.

We interviewed end users and built two personas for the distinct user types in the ACO-MS ecosystem, drawn from real interviews, not assumptions.

Persona one

ACO administrator

Manages registration, compliance, and reporting for the organization. Lives in the deadlines and the paperwork, and needs to know exactly where every application stands.

Persona two

Healthcare provider

Needs fast, verified access to the system without the federal overhead. Comes in to get something specific done, then get back to care.

Personas built from real interviews, the two mental models the redesign had to serve.

What it told us

01

Process was too long

Registration and NOIA verification made users navigate too many disconnected steps with unclear progress signals. They didn't know how far they were from done.

02

Waiting without feedback

Users had no visibility into where their application stood, no status indicators, no estimated timelines. The silence after submission created anxiety and repeat contacts to support.

03

Visual complexity overload

The existing system lacked padding, spacing, and hierarchy, making it hard to focus or scan key information on dense federal forms.

Wireframes and user research

Low-fidelity first, validated before a single pixel of hi-fi.

I started with wireframes to build structure, running daily standups with the product manager and lead business analyst. The low-fidelity flow drove usability testing before any visual decisions, validating assumptions cheaply.

Low-fidelity wireframes, structure validated before hi-fi.

Users were positive about the direction and gave feedback that shaped the final flows. We ran think-aloud sessions, where users completed tasks while narrating their reasoning.

Wireframe usability testing results.

Branding

Visual design aligned with CMS culture.

Two goals: match CMS's existing brand culture so ACO-MS felt like a trusted government product, and fix the visual clarity problems that made the old system exhausting. I used Adobe Color to derive a palette from CMS's official standards, then applied the spacing, type hierarchy, and component rules the system had never had.

Program performance results

Measurable impact on Medicare beneficiaries.

Per CMS 2022 performance results, the platform supported 66 new enrollments, 140 renewals, and over 11 million beneficiaries receiving care, a 3% growth of 324K vs the prior year. The redesign contributed directly by removing the registration barriers that were keeping new ACOs from onboarding.
