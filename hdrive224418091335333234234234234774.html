Background

Healthcare data in a briefcase

As our population grows and ages, holistic healthcare becomes increasingly important to longevity and quality of life. Technology provides an ever-increasing stream of health data — but disparate systems track each piece of the equation separately, lacking the whole-person, longitudinal perspective that would truly benefit people.

The Problem

Fragmented systems, no whole-person view

Disparate systems track different pieces of the healthcare equation — EHR, claims, wearables, insurance — but none bring them together. People lack a unified, longitudinal view of their own health data, and providers can't collaborate across systems effectively.

The Solution

One platform that unifies everything

HDrive brings together citizens, providers, physician groups, and hospitals to securely store and exchange health data — including EHR, encounter data, claims, and quality data. Combined with wearable data and AI, it delivers holistic health insights for every individual. Health data in a briefcase.

Validating the Idea

A structured process across both phases

The application was built in Beta and Alpha phases. During Beta, the core MVP was designed and developed. In Alpha, the remaining features were built out. Across both phases, I followed a research-driven process to validate assumptions before any screen was finalized.

Design process across Beta and Alpha phases

As the only designer, I gathered data from internal stakeholders and the product manager to understand client needs and business goals — then moved into competitive analysis, user personas, information architecture, and wireframing before validating with usability tests.

Objective 02

As part of the Alpha product, the objective was to incorporate the remaining features of the platform. This phase expanded on the Beta MVP with the full feature set, complete information architecture, validated user flows, and 508-compliant hi-fidelity prototypes.

Information Architecture

With all features ready to implement, I worked closely with the Product Manager to iterate and validate the information architecture. I conducted a card sorting exercise and user surveys with 8 stakeholders and synthesized the insights. Key finding: users didn't like having all modules in their own separate section — they preferred grouped navigation. This feedback also informed the design system direction.

Next phase roadmap and validation plan

HDrive demonstrated that a single, dedicated UX designer — armed with a rigorous research process — can take a complex healthcare data platform from a proof of concept through two full shipped phases. The strongest design outcome was not any individual screen but the unified information architecture that made a multi-user, multi-data-source platform feel navigable and coherent.
